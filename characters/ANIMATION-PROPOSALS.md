# Puppy animation proposals

Proposed movements for the puppy described in [the character direction](README.md), using [the current static model](puppy/README.md) as the visual basis. These describe target movements, not a commitment to build every movement. [Q1–Q4 quiet-animation drafts and a test app](puppy/animations/README.md) are now implemented for review; the remaining movements are proposals. The subsequently authorised [rig prototype](puppy/rig/README.md) provides controls, diagnostic poses and a coverage/limitations table for these requirements. The aim is a warm, attentive companion whose reactions support the learner without competing with the lesson.

## Timing and movement conventions

- Durations include anticipation, the main movement and recovery. Except for the breathing loop, each clip starts and finishes in the neutral four-paw standing pose with a gentle smile, unless explicitly described as a layer.
- Angles and distances are starting points for visual experiments, not anatomical limits. Distances use the puppy's own dimensions; “body height” means ground to shoulder. Left/right refer to the puppy. Head tilt means an ear towards a shoulder; head turn means looking left/right; nod means muzzle up/down.
- Keep the paws planted during upper-body gestures. Small movements should read in the face and silhouette at the intended display size; increase clarity before increasing speed or exaggeration.
- “Layer” means the movement can affect just its named body parts while another compatible clip plays. “Full body” means the clip owns the whole pose, including expression. Do not add a second tail wag, head turn or facial expression on top of a clip already controlling that part.
- A gentle closed-mouth smile is the default throughout quiet gameplay and both success categories, including their entry and recovery poses. Failure reactions briefly soften the smile to a sympathetic, relaxed mouth, then restore it during encouragement. Smiling is a baseline expression, not a standalone success clip.
- Facial descriptions are deliberately cartoon-like: a smile is a subtle lift of the mouth corners and cheeks, not a human grin or exposed teeth. Avoid angry brows, eye rolling or prolonged sadness.

## Quiet gameplay

Most of the time the puppy should simply breathe. Other gestures are occasional alternatives, separated by irregular pauses; this is not a continuous playlist. Save scratching for a natural pause in the activity.

## Minor success

Choose one clear, cheerful reaction for an ordinary correct answer: a happy tail wag, play bow or little jump. The puppy keeps its default smile throughout; the body movement supplies the emphasis. A smile or small nod alone does not count as a success reaction.

| **MIN2. Back slopes down towards shoulders, neck lifts enough to keep the eyes towards the learner. Tail lifts and wags.                                                           | Full body. ## Major success

Reserve these bigger, unmistakable celebrations for meaningful milestones, with enough screen space and time to finish. Choose one rather than playing them consecutively. The ears-up happy surprise offers a major reaction without a travelling or airborne stunt, but must visibly change the whole silhouette to earn that role.

Safe to fade after the tall hold as the body settles. |

## Failure, encouragement and recovery

The puppy briefly softens its usual smile to share an “oh” moment, then restores it while communicating readiness to try again. Keep the sympathetic part short (usually under a second) and avoid judgement, exaggerated distress or a defeated exit pose. Each row is a complete alternative response, not three clips to play consecutively after every mistake.

Inner brow ends rise a little, with mouth relaxed. Then head returns, ears regain their neutral shape, mouth forms a small smile and tail gives one slow sweep. ## What this asks of the rig

These are movement capabilities to test, not a prescribed bone count. Start with the smallest rig that can make the everyday clips appealing; use the harder poses to decide whether more controls or topology changes are justified.

| Area               | Minimum proposed capability                                                                                                                                                               | Movements that expose the limit                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Whole character    | A root for position and rotation, distinct from local pelvis/chest motion. Keep each full-body clip's final position/facing predictable.                                                  | Jump height/landing (MIN3), turning steps (MAJ1), full backward rotation (MAJ2).                                                                                                                                                         |
| Torso and neck     | Independently position pelvis and chest; bend spine forwards and sideways; turn, tilt and nod the head without distorting the muzzle. A small breathing deformation may be enough for Q1. | Bow (MIN2), scratch (Q6), looking back during chase (MAJ1).                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Four legs and paws | Independently bend each leg and orient each paw. Authoring controls should make planted contacts easy; shoulders/hips need enough freedom to fold without collapse.                       | Three-paw support and scratch reach (Q6), crouch/tuck/landing (MIN3/MAJ2), stepping (MAJ1). Toe articulation is not requested.                                                                                                           |
| Eyes and lids      | Modest independent eye aiming, full blink closure and a gentle squint. Clamp gaze to a range that keeps eyes enclosed.                                                                    | Look around (Q4), widened happy-surprise eyes (MAJ3), blink during recovery (F3). No pupil resizing is requested.                                                                                                                        |
| Brows              | Raise either whole brow and, if useful, the inner ends separately. Keep each solid brow embedded as it travels over the curved forehead.                                                  | Asymmetric curiosity (Q3), happy-surprise brow lift (MAJ3), sympathy (F1). A straight vertical translation is not sufficient evidence of safe movement on this mesh.                                                                     |
| Mouth and cheeks   | Default closed-mouth smile, with corners lifted roughly 2–4% of muzzle width and cheeks following; broaden for delight or soften to a relaxed mouth for failure, then restore the smile.  | Baseline expression, broader delight (MAJ3) and all failure/recovery transitions. Test topology around the current mouth before choosing morph targets or bones. Jaw opening, teeth, tongue and speech shapes are outside this proposal. |
| Ears               | Move each ear root independently, bend its floppy length and unfold it into a tall, near-upright pose; retain a soft delayed tip response.                                                | Twitch (Q5), contact during scratch (Q6), landing (MIN3), tall ears and folding recovery (MAJ3). Bake follow-through rather than requiring runtime simulation.                                                                           |
| Tail               | Lift/lower the base and bend the length into a smooth curve; let the tip lag a wag.                                                                                                       | MIN1, MAJ1 and F1. Keep the existing continuous taper; avoid rigid stick-like swinging or sharp hinges.                                                                                                                                  |

The static mesh is a visual basis, not yet verified deformation topology. In particular, bending the torso/legs, closing lids, shaping the muzzle and moving the deeply embedded brows need pose tests. Separate facial pieces must not detach visually or expose gaps as expressions change. Any Blender-only constraints should ultimately be baked into the exported bone/morph animation described in the [production direction](README.md).

## Clip combinations and interruption

- Breathing can sit beneath a blink, gaze or small expression when those clips do not also move the chest. Pause or reduce it during a full-body clip that already animates breathing/compression.
- A blink can usually accompany a head turn or tail wag. Expressions that already shape the eyelids need a combined pose or explicit blending rule so two layers do not over-close the eyes. Suppress random gaze/ear gestures during authored reactions.
- Ordinary answer feedback should take priority over ambient gestures. Fade out a small tilt or twitch over roughly 0.2–0.4 s; finish lowering a scratching paw before switching. Keep only the latest relevant pending reaction rather than replaying a backlog.
- Do not interrupt an airborne jump/flip or a turning step with a standing pose. Mark stable contact points and completion of the landing; transition once the puppy has support. Learning controls should remain responsive while a protected movement finishes.
- Keep the character within its allotted stage, especially for chase and flip. Return to the same anchor and facing through actual steps/settling, not an abrupt end-of-clip reset. Check ear and tail reach as well as body bounds.
- For reduced motion, use a quiet smiling pose and a gentle expression change for feedback; replace MAJ3 with a modest brow lift and broader smile, omitting its ear spring, shimmy and repeated wag; omit chase, flip, jump, scratch and repeated ambient motion. Stop unnecessary rendering while hidden, and resume in a stable pose without replaying missed celebrations. These are proposed controller requirements, not implemented behaviour.

## Suggested review order

1. **Everyday expression sample:** Q1/Q2, Q3 and MIN1, with the default smile and complete F1 recovery. This tests whether blinking, brows, smiling, head motion and wagging are readable and friendly with the current face.
2. **Body deformation sample:** MIN2 and MIN3 to test planted feet, spine bending, compression and landing. Check from front, side and three-quarter views, not just the hero camera.
3. **Major expression sample:** MAJ3 to test the tall ear silhouette, happy-surprise face and supporting body motion. Check that it reads as a major celebration beside MIN1–MIN3, and that the ears can unfold and recover without looking rigid or intersecting the head.
4. **Complexity probes:** block out Q6, MAJ1 and MAJ2 before polishing them. Scratch tests reach and support, chase tests lateral bending and steps, and flip tests clearance and landing. Decide individually whether their value justifies the additional authoring work. Omitting these three still leaves a useful everyday library.

At the next authorised animation prototype, check neutral and extreme poses for brow/head intersections, exposed eyeballs, muzzle gaps, collapsing joints, ear/body collisions and foot sliding. Review appeal and readability at the intended on-screen size, then test exported clips and transitions on the oldest supported iPad once that device is specified. The prototype should establish the required deformation quality and control range before fixing the final rig complexity or building all clips.
